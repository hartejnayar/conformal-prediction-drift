## 2. Cross-paper synthesis

### What is already established in this five-paper set?

1. **Online adaptation to changing distributions is well studied.** DtACI and strongly adaptive online conformal prediction both address temporal change, but with different algorithmic ideas and theoretical objectives.
2. **Coverage and prediction-set efficiency have already been studied jointly.** Bhatnagar et al. report improvements in coverage and set size; Srinivas explicitly studies a Pareto trade-off between coverage mistakes and interval length; Vaze’s 2026 preprint directly proposes simultaneous coverage and efficiency guarantees. Therefore, “we compare efficiency versus reliability” is not a sufficient novelty claim.
3. **Covariate shift is a distinct setting from arbitrary temporal/concept drift.** JAWS-X uses SCS/FCS assumptions and weighted jackknife+-style methods. It should not be put into the same direct leaderboard as online methods unless the experiment establishes a valid common setting and respects each method’s assumptions.
4. **“Efficiency” needs to be operationally defined.** At least three dimensions are present in this literature: (i) statistical efficiency / interval width or set size, (ii) computational efficiency / number of model fits or wall-clock runtime, and (iii) temporal efficiency / regret or speed of recovering coverage after a shift. Do not collapse them into one score without a justified definition.
5. **Guarantees and empirical results are different evidence.** A theoretical marginal coverage bound under assumptions is not the same as observed empirical coverage in a finite experiment; local coverage, signed long-run error, and absolute non-cancelling violation are not interchangeable metrics.

### Candidate research directions to investigate (not confirmed gaps)

- **Direction A — Coverage recovery after drift:** Compare selected online methods on matched abrupt and gradual shift schedules; report local coverage, absolute coverage violation, interval width, and time-to-recovery. First verify overlap with Bhatnagar and Vaze.
- **Direction B — Multi-dimensional efficiency:** Among methods valid under the same assumptions, compare interval width, model-fit count, and wall-clock time on the same base predictor and hardware. This is narrower than “efficiency vs reliability,” but novelty remains unproven.
- **Direction C — Feedback information and adaptability:** Examine how performance changes when methods receive only binary coverage feedback versus richer conformity-score feedback, with a clearly stated benchmark and guarantees. Check Srinivas and Vaze carefully before selecting this direction.

### Recommended provisional narrowing

Do **not** keep the title as a final title yet. First choose one coherent setting:

- **Online temporal drift:** DtACI, strongly adaptive online CP, and other methods designed for online sequences; or
- **Covariate / feedback shift:** JAWS-X and methods whose weighting assumptions are appropriate.

A single study spanning covariate shift and arbitrary concept drift may become unfair or incoherent if methods rely on incompatible assumptions. If both settings remain, treat them as separate experimental tracks rather than a single undifferentiated ranking.

## 3. Evidence-quality labels for the literature review

Use these labels in later notes and drafts:

- **[THEOREM]** Formal result with assumptions checked against the paper.
- **[EXPERIMENT]** Reported result in the authors’ tested setup.
- **[AUTHOR LIMITATION]** Limitation explicitly stated by the authors.
- **[READER INFERENCE]** A cautious interpretation by us, not a claim made by the paper.
- **[VERIFY]** Detail still needs full-text confirmation.
